## How to run

See "Run it" in the README. M5 added four scripts and extended the report. `eval/mqm.py` and `eval/consistency.py` call the judge model (`JUDGE_*` in `.env`, billed apart from the translation model); the rest read files only:

```bash
uv run python eval/mqm.py runs/eval/<id>                  # GEMBA-MQM over a finished eval run: mqm-<model>.json
uv run python eval/mqm.py --reparse runs/eval/<id>/mqm-<model>.json --dry-run   # re-read stored answers; no call
uv run python eval/consistency.py runs/eval/<id>          # term consistency: consistency-<model>.json
uv run python eval/mqm_sample.py draw --baseline <run> <run> --full <run> <run> --seed 0   # the human agreement sample
uv run python eval/mqm_agreement.py runs/mqm-human/<time>-sample --expected-sha256 <hash> <mqm file> <mqm file>
uv run python eval/report.py runs/eval/<id> ... --mqm-judge <model> --mqm-agreement <agreement file>   # pooled report
```

A dev pass of the tuned configs is four `eval/run.py` runs as in M4 (`full_no_kb` and `full`, one per book), then `eval/terms.py`, `eval/comet.py`, `eval/consistency.py` and `eval/mqm.py` on each run's directory, then the report.

## Design decisions

The reasons are in [OUTLINE.md §8](../OUTLINE.md#8-decisions). The short form:

- **A judge of another provider, billed apart.** GPT-4o mini (`gpt-4o-mini-2024-07-18`) goes through the same `LLMClient` with its own configuration, Meter and ledger. Its spend is the evaluation's, not the system's, and counts against the $100 cap. OpenAI shuts the model down on 2027-01-14, so every judge call has to happen before.
- **The decision rule before the judge.** The labels were written, and their hash recorded, before any judge output on the sample existed, and the thresholds (τ_b at least 0.30; at least 60% of the annotator's major or critical errors found) were fixed before that. The fallback judge, GPT-5.1, was fixed in advance too.
- **Term accuracy stays primary.** The dev runs showed that term accuracy cannot see what the KB is for. Switching the primary metric after that null result would look like choosing a metric after seeing results, so term consistency is added as a secondary metric, and the protocol says that this hypothesis was formed after the dev runs.
- **A second `baseline` pass** on both dev books measures how far two runs of one config differ.
- **One round of tuning, with a keep rule.** The tuning is kept unless `full`'s pooled COMET or primary term accuracy falls by more than the largest per-book spread of the two `baseline` passes. The rule was fixed before the tuned runs.

## The agreement check

The sample is 30 segments drawn from the dev runs' `baseline` and `full` outputs, shown without their config, with only the source and the translation. **The annotator is Claude, an LLM, and not a person**: the author does not read Japanese and decided to have a fresh Claude session annotate the sample. That session was told to read only the guide and the sheet, and its tool calls show that it opened nothing else. The check therefore measures a judge's agreement with another LLM, which is weaker evidence than agreement with a person.

| Judge | τ_b | Best τ_b its ties allowed | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| GPT-4o mini | −0.042 | 0.305 | fail |
| GPT-5.1 (the fallback) | 0.283 | 0.293 | fail |

**Both judges failed, so MQM is reported for reference only**, with GPT-4o mini's scores. How strong that is: the annotator marked an error on 1 of the 30 items (a minor addition) and none on the other 29, so there was no major or critical error to find and τ_b decided alone. Of the 435 pairs of items only the 29 pairs that include the marked item can be concordant or discordant. GPT-4o mini had 8 concordant and 12 discordant pairs. GPT-5.1 had 28 concordant and none discordant, but it gave many items the same score, which capped its τ_b at 0.293, under the threshold. A fail on these labels is weak evidence against a judge, not proof that its errors are wrong. A person's labels, or a sample with more marked errors, would be needed to tell the two judges apart.

For scale: on the `baseline` run of the dev books (pass A) GPT-4o mini marked at least one error in 514 of 606 segments (948 errors, 238 of them critical), and its pooled MQM scores are −12 to −13 for every config. GPT-5.1's scores on the same runs were between −2.5 and −3.1. The MQM scores are in the report, but the dev numbers below do not rest on them.

One parser rule came out of the check. GPT-5.1 sometimes answers with only a line "No-error: …" and no severity heading, which the parser, being stricter than GEMBA's, had treated as unreadable (11 of 274 segments of one run). The author chose to read such an answer as no error, as GEMBA does, and the stored answers were re-parsed without a new call (`eval/mqm.py --reparse`). The rule was set before GPT-5.1's agreement was computed.

Term consistency was +13.6 pp for `full`, against a pooled spread of 7.0 pp. M4's reading that `full` shows no gain on the reference-based metrics holds.

## The tuning

**What was read.** 15 pages of each dev book (30 pages) of the M4 `full` runs, against the Japanese and 9 of the page images. The tuning's evidence is this reading, not the judge, since the judge had failed the agreement check; the MQM file served only to choose pages. The rule, written in a log before any prompt changed: every page not approved on the first review (13), every page the MQM file lists for `full`'s additions (the category where `full` stood out), the pages with the most grammar marks, and one page added while reading. The reader was the Executor, an LLM agent that reads Japanese. Nobody who reads Japanese has checked the reading; the independent review re-checked six cited segments against the stored Japanese and they agreed. 7 patterns were acted on and 11 were not. Among the 28 approved pages read, 9 carried an error the Reviewer should have sent back (5 approved on the first review, 4 after a Send-back); one of the latter, a mistranslation, was introduced by the review loop itself.

**What changed** (text only; no code, argument schema or other tool description):

- The Translator's prompts: split a sentence that runs across several regions instead of repeating it or turning a question into a statement; add nothing the Japanese leaves out; check the earlier pages before choosing characters for a kana name the page does not settle.
- The Reviewer's prompts: what counts as an addition; read regions that share a sentence together; send a page back only for wrong meaning, wrong referent, omissions, additions or a lost question, not for wording; do not send back over a referent only earlier pages could settle; keep what was right in a suggestion.
- The revision message: the Reviewer sees one page and can be wrong, so the Translator checks each issue before following it.
- The KB text only: lookups match exactly, aliases point to one person, a known person's other name is rendered consistently with their profile.
- The Curator's prompt: reject a target fitted to one sentence, and ordinary compounds.

Every passage that the KB-on and KB-off prompts share changed in both, word for word, so the ablation stays fair, and a script checked that no changed text names a dev book, character or glossary term. The plain `translator` prompt and `baseline` did not change. `backend/tests/test_kb_seams.py` pins the 16 request digests again; it fails when a prompt word, a tool-schema description or the request layout changes. No test pins the Curator's prompt (`IDEAS.md`).

## What the numbers say

One run per config and no confidence intervals (the protocol's paired bootstrap is M8). The spread above is one sample. The prompts were tuned by reading these two books and the runs are on the same books, so **none of this is a held-out estimate**; the test books are untouched. With those cautions:

- **`full` vs `baseline` (primary metric COMET):** no gain. Tuned `full` is 0.0003 lower on COMET, 0.70 higher on chrF and 0.03 lower on BLEU; the spread is 0.0007, 0.55 and 0.81 pooled, and up to 0.0037, 1.31 and 1.68 in a book. `full` costs about 9.5 times as much per page and takes about 5.5 times as long (p50; 7 times before the tuning).
- **`full` vs `full_no_kb` (primary metric term accuracy):** after the tuning, `full` is ahead: 73 against 67 of 117 (+5.1 pp), by 3 occurrences in each book. In M4 the two were equal (61 against 61). The spread of term accuracy is 1.7 pp pooled and up to 3.7 pp in a book, so the gap is larger than the one sample of the spread, but it is 6 occurrences.
- **Term consistency (secondary):** `full` 83.2% against `full_no_kb` 72.2% (+11.0 pp; +9.6 pp in M4). The spread is 7.0 pp pooled. This is the effect the KB is for, and it was seen before the tuning too. The score is strict: two renderings count as one only when they are the same after normalisation, and if renderings that contain one another counted as the same, the M4 consistency would be 80.8% for `baseline`, 79.2% for `full_no_kb` and 91.2% for `full`. `full` still leads; `baseline` and `full_no_kb` swap places.
- **Where the tuning's gain is.** Primary term accuracy of `full` rose from 61 to 73 of 117, and all of the rise is in `boureisougi` (31 to 43 of 54); `tojime_no_siora` is unchanged (30 of 63). `full_no_kb` rose by 6, also only in `boureisougi` (34 to 40). So half of `full`'s rise appears without the KB and comes from the prompts both configs share; the other half could be the KB or sampling.
- **COMET and chrF** moved by no more than the largest per-book spread (0.0037 and 1.31) in either tuned config, against its M4 run.
- **MQM** (reference only): −12.22 for tuned `full`, −12.80 for tuned `full_no_kb`, −12.17 for `baseline` (pass A).

The keep rule gave "keep": COMET rose by 0.0019 against a threshold of 0.0037, and primary term accuracy by 10.3 pp against a threshold of 3.7 pp; neither fell.
